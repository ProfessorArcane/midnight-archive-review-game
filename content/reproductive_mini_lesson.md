# Reproductive System & Pharmacy Connections

**PHRA 1001 supplemental mini-lesson (proposed 25–30 minutes).** Teach before enabling these questions in a weekly round. These are original items, not official NHA exam questions.

## Learning goals
Students identify basic male and female reproductive structures; distinguish ovulation from fertilization; recognize common contraceptive routes, ingredients and brand/generic pairs; and refer clinical counseling questions to the pharmacist.

## Instructor sequence
1. **Connect (3 min):** Reproductive health is part of pharmacy practice: prescriptions include contraceptives, hormone therapies and medications affecting pregnancy.
2. **Identify anatomy (6 min):** Ovaries produce ova and reproductive hormones; fallopian tubes are the usual site of fertilization; uterus supports pregnancy; testes produce sperm; epididymis stores/matures sperm; vas deferens transports sperm.
3. **Explain physiology (4 min):** LH surge triggers ovulation. Avoid reducing the reproductive system to only fertility or pregnancy.
4. **Connect to medications (6 min):** Discuss oral pills, vaginal rings, patches, injections and intrauterine devices as different routes/forms. Match Plan B One-Step to levonorgestrel and Sprintec to ethinyl estradiol/norgestimate. Explain that products sharing an ingredient may have very different uses, doses and routes.
5. **Safety (3 min):** Technicians can identify products and clarify label/insurance data but should refer patient-specific clinical questions to the pharmacist.
6. **Game quiz (5–8 min):** Use the 10 original reproductive items in the accompanying JSON. Reveal answer and explanation after each; do not use before teaching.

## Quality control
Verify local scope-of-practice requirements and current product labeling prior to classroom deployment. Keep reproductive anatomy items supplemental to the pharmacy-focused ExCPT blueprint unless mapped to an actual lesson.
