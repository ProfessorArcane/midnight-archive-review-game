### Questions 1–5

```text
1. SIG · Route, frequency and quantity
Category: Foundations / Prescription Intake
NHA blueprint: K.80, K.111; Domain 4A/4C
Week placement: PENDING INSTRUCTOR CONFIRMATION
Question: A pharmacy technician receives a prescription for a medication with the directions '1 tab po bid x 14 days.' How many tablets are required to fill the order?
A. 14 tablets
B. 28 tablets
C. 30 tablets
D. 42 tablets
Correct answer: B
Explanation: One tablet per dose × two doses each day × 14 days = 28 tablets. 'po' means by mouth and 'bid' means twice daily.
Origin: Existing SIG topic; rewritten as a realistic four-option pharmacy calculation.
Status: DRAFT FOR INDEPENDENT CHECK, NOT LIVE

2. Cardiovascular · Drug classification
Category: Therapeutics / Cardiovascular
NHA blueprint: K.57, K.61, K.62, K.63; Domain 3A
Week placement: PENDING INSTRUCTOR CONFIRMATION
Question: While preparing a prescription for lisinopril 10 mg, a technician is asked what therapeutic class the medication belongs to. Which class should the technician recognize?
A. Angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor
B. Angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB)
C. Calcium channel blocker
D. Beta-adrenergic blocker
Correct answer: A
Explanation: Lisinopril is an ACE inhibitor commonly prescribed for hypertension. The technician may identify the class but refers patient-specific counseling questions to the pharmacist.
Origin: Body-system draft; converted to pharmacy-setting multiple choice.
Status: DRAFT FOR INDEPENDENT CHECK, NOT LIVE

3. Respiratory · Brand/generic
Category: Therapeutics / Respiratory
NHA blueprint: K.67, K.68; Domain 3B
Week placement: PENDING INSTRUCTOR CONFIRMATION
Question: A prescription is written for Ventolin HFA. Which generic drug name should the technician recognize when selecting the corresponding product?
A. Fluticasone
B. Montelukast
C. Albuterol
D. Ipratropium
Correct answer: C
Explanation: Ventolin HFA is a brand of albuterol inhalation aerosol. Fluticasone, montelukast and ipratropium are different respiratory medications.
Origin: Body-system draft; brand/generic recognition.
Status: DRAFT FOR INDEPENDENT CHECK, NOT LIVE

4. Endocrine · Medication class
Category: Therapeutics / Endocrine
NHA blueprint: K.57, K.61, K.62; Domain 3A
Week placement: PENDING INSTRUCTOR CONFIRMATION
Question: A prescription for metformin is entered for a patient with type 2 diabetes. Which drug class should the technician associate with metformin?
A. Sulfonylurea
B. DPP-4 inhibitor
C. SGLT2 inhibitor
D. Biguanide
Correct answer: D
Explanation: Metformin is a biguanide. Sulfonylureas include glipizide; DPP-4 inhibitors include sitagliptin; SGLT2 inhibitors include empagliflozin.
Origin: Student whiteboard and body-system draft; class recognition.
Status: DRAFT FOR INDEPENDENT CHECK, NOT LIVE

5. Reproductive · Similar ingredient, different product
Category: Systems / Reproductive
NHA blueprint: K.59, K.60, K.68; Domain 3A/3B
Week placement: PENDING INSTRUCTOR CONFIRMATION
Question: Two products, Plan B One-Step and Mirena, both contain levonorgestrel. Which statement most accurately differentiates them?
A. They are both oral products used in the same dosing regimen.
B. Plan B One-Step is an oral emergency contraceptive; Mirena is an intrauterine contraceptive system.
C. Mirena is an oral emergency contraceptive; Plan B One-Step is an intrauterine system.
D. They use the same dosage form but have different brand names.
Correct answer: B
Explanation: Both contain levonorgestrel but have different dosage forms, administration routes and intended uses. Product-selection or counseling questions must go to the pharmacist.

### Questions 6–10

```text
6. Controlled substances · DEA arithmetic
Category: Law / Controlled substances
NHA blueprint: K.47; Domain 2B
Week placement: PENDING INSTRUCTOR CONFIRMATION
Question: A fictional DEA registration example is written as AB123456?. When applying the standard checksum arithmetic to the six digits, which check digit is expected?
A. 1
B. 2
C. 3
D. 8
Correct answer: C
Explanation: Odd positions: 1+3+5=9. Even positions: 2+4+6=12; double that to 24. Total 33, giving 3 as the check digit. Matching the check digit alone does not establish that a registration is valid.
Origin: Student's recalled exam topic; arithmetic item rewritten unambiguously.
Status: DRAFT FOR INDEPENDENT CHECK, NOT LIVE

7. Patient confidentiality · Caller request
Category: Patient Care / Privacy
NHA blueprint: K.25; Domain 2A
Week placement: PENDING INSTRUCTOR CONFIRMATION
Question: An unverified caller says she is a patient's adult daughter and asks the pharmacy to read the patient's medication list because the patient is in the emergency department. What is the technician's best initial action?
A. Read the list because a family relationship was claimed.
B. Read only the names of controlled substances.
C. Decline to disclose immediately, follow pharmacy verification procedures, and refer the request to the pharmacist.
D. Send the medication list to any number the caller provides.
Correct answer: C
Explanation: The technician should not disclose PHI to an unverified caller. HIPAA permits some disclosures to family or providers under defined conditions, so the pharmacist and established verification workflow should determine what is permitted.
Origin: Revises existing live scenario to remove over-absolute HIPAA interpretation.
Status: DRAFT FOR INDEPENDENT CHECK, NOT LIVE

8. Pharmacy business math · Markup
Category: Practice / Calculations
NHA blueprint: K.122; Domain 4C
Week placement: PENDING INSTRUCTOR CONFIRMATION
Question: A pharmacy buys an item for $30 and sells it for $40. What is the percentage markup based on acquisition cost? Round to the nearest tenth of a percent.
A. 25.0%
B. 30.0%
C. 33.3%
D. 40.0%
Correct answer: C
Explanation: Markup on cost = ($40−$30)/$30×100 = 33.3%. Gross profit margin on selling price would be 25.0%, which answers a different question.
Origin: Student's recalled exam scenario, with markup wording corrected.
Status: DRAFT FOR INDEPENDENT CHECK, NOT LIVE

9. Packaging · Classroom conversion
Category: Practice / Calculations
NHA blueprint: K.108, K.115; Domain 4C
Week placement: PENDING INSTRUCTOR CONFIRMATION
Question: A liquid prescription requires 150 mL. For this question, use the pharmacy classroom conversion 1 fl oz = 30 mL. How many 1-fl-oz bottles are needed to package the entire quantity?
A. 4 bottles
B. 5 bottles
C. 6 bottles
D. 7 bottles
Correct answer: B
Explanation: 150 mL÷30 mL per bottle = 5 bottles using the stipulated approximation. If exact U.S. fluid-ounce capacity is intended instead, it must be explicitly stated and whole containers rounded up.
Origin: Student's recalled exam scenario, with the essential conversion assumption supplied.

10. Sterile compounding · USP chapter
Category: Integration / Pharmacy Workflow
NHA blueprint: K.125, K.130; Domain 4D

A. USP <795>
B. USP <800>
C. USP <797>
D. USP <825>
Correct answer: C
Explanation: USP <797> sets standards for sterile compounding. USP <795> addresses nonsterile compounding; USP <800> covers hazardous-drug handling; USP <825> covers radiopharmaceutical preparations.
